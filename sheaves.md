**Description:** This is a graduate course on homological algebra and its applications in geometry and topology.  The first half covers the basics of homological algebra, focusing on categories of sheaves: sheaves on topological spaces, abelian categories, derived functors, sheaf cohomology and its comparison with de Rham, Čech and singular cohomology, and spectral sequences.  The second half covers derived categories and their applications: triangulated categories, derived functors on the derived category, the six functors for sheaves on topological spaces, Verdier duality, and a first look at perverse sheaves and derived equivalences.

**Prerequisites:** Commutative algebra (PMATH 446) and algebraic topology (PMATH 467).  Having taken last year's PMATH 945 (Category Theory and Homological Algebra, W26) would be ideal, but isn't required.  I'll assume you know basic category theory, point-set and (preferably) algebraic topology, and modules over rings.
- To prepare for the class, the first 4 chapters of [Schapira's notes on Categories and Homological Algebra](https://perso.imj-prg.fr/pierre-schapira/wp-content/uploads/schapira-pub/lectnotes/HomAl.pdf) would be a good start, as would Chapters 1–3 and Appendices B, C of [Wedhorn's *Manifolds, Sheaves, and Cohomology*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-658-10633-1).  Appendix D of Wedhorn is a good reference for basic homological algebra.  
- For algebraic topology, Chapters 2–3 of [Hatcher's *Algebraic Topology*](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html) are the relevant background.  
- If you want to review category theory beforehand, [Leinster's *Basic Category Theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09375) or [Riehl's *Category Theory in Context*](https://math.jhu.edu/~eriehl/context/) are both good choices.

*Part I: Homological algebra through sheaves*

- Feb. 9: Čech cohomology; Leray's theorem; computations
- Feb. 11: Comparison with singular cohomology; homotopy invariance

- Feb. 23: Spectral sequences
- Feb. 25: Higher direct images; the Grothendieck and Leray spectral sequences

*Part II: Derived categories and applications*

- Mar. 2: Why complexes? The homotopy category; cones and distinguished triangles
- Mar. 4: Triangulated categories; localization at quasi-isomorphisms
- Mar. 9: The derived category; Hom in the derived category is Ext; truncation
- Mar. 11: Right derived functors on the derived category; composition of derived functors
- Mar. 16: Left derived functors; derived tensor product; derived adjunctions and the projection formula
- Mar. 18: Compact supports; proper base change; the Künneth formula
- Mar. 23: Verdier duality; the dualizing complex
- Mar. 25: Consequences of duality: Poincaré and Alexander duality
- Mar. 30: Constructible sheaves; t-structures; perverse sheaves
- Apr. 1: Derived equivalences: tilting and derived Morita theory
- Apr. 6: Limitations of triangulated categories; dg-enhancements; outlook
